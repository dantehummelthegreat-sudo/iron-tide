# Customer Research — 6-Week Body Transformation Challenge

**Avatar:** Everyday adults, 30–50, out of shape, have tried and failed to get fit
before, feel self-conscious and stuck. This is the foundation document for all
landing page copy. Quotes below are written in the customer's own voice — use them
as raw material for headlines, bullets, and objection handling.

---

## 1. Top 5 Emotional Pains (Body + Past Failed Attempts)

### Pain 1 — The stranger in the mirror / in photos
They avoid mirrors, untag photos, and dread the candid shot a friend posts. There's
a gap between how old they feel inside (still 25) and the person looking back.
- *"I caught my reflection and didn't recognize myself."*
- *"I'm the one always taking the picture so I'm never in it."*
- *"When did I become this person?"*

### Pain 2 — Shame disguised as "I'm fine"
Deep embarrassment they rarely say out loud — about their weight, their breathing
on the stairs, needing the bigger size. It shows up as self-deprecating jokes and
quiet withdrawal, not complaints.
- *"I laugh it off but it kills me."*
- *"I don't want anyone to see how far I've let it go."*
- *"I'm embarrassed to even walk into a gym looking like this."*

### Pain 3 — The graveyard of failed attempts (and the self-blame)
Keto, Peloton collecting dust, the gym membership they paid for and stopped using,
Whole30, the app streak they broke on day 9. Each failure compounds into "the
problem is me." They don't trust themselves to finish anything anymore.
- *"I always start strong and then life happens."*
- *"I've lost the same 20 pounds five times."*
- *"What's the point, I'll just quit again like always."*

### Pain 4 — Physical decline they can't ignore anymore
This is the new, scary part that wasn't there at 25. Knees, back, sleep, energy,
a doctor's comment, a blood-pressure reading. The body is sending bills.
- *"I'm winded just playing with my kids."*
- *"My doctor used the word 'pre-diabetic' and it scared me."*
- *"I wake up tired and I'm tired all day."*

### Pain 5 — Feeling invisible, stuck, and like time is running out
A low-grade grief that the window is closing. They've prioritized everyone else —
kids, spouse, job — and disappeared in the process. Stuck, not failing
dramatically, just slowly fading.
- *"I've spent ten years taking care of everyone but me."*
- *"If I don't do something now, when?"*
- *"I just feel stuck and I don't know how to start."*

## 2. Deepest Desires — What Success Actually Looks/Feels Like

It is **almost never about a number on a scale.** The scale is the proxy; the real
prize is identity and capability. Sell the feeling, not the pounds.

- **To feel like themselves again** — "I want to feel like me again," recognizing
  the person in the mirror, the old confidence coming back.
- **Energy and capability** — keeping up with their kids, hiking without stopping,
  getting off the floor easily, sleeping through the night, no afternoon crash.
- **Confidence in their own skin** — putting on a shirt/dress and liking what they
  see, beach without a coverup, walking into a room without sucking it in.
- **To finally finish something** — proof to themselves they can start AND complete
  a hard thing. The win is psychological as much as physical: "I did it."
- **To be there for the long haul** — being around and active for their kids/
  grandkids; not becoming their parents' health story; fear-of-death reframed as
  love.
- **To stop thinking about it** — the constant background guilt and self-monitoring
  finally going quiet. Peace.

**What success FEELS like (use this language):** lighter, energized, proud,
"clothes fit again," "I have my mornings back," waking up rested, standing taller,
"my husband noticed," "my kids said I'm more fun now."

## 3. Top 5 Objections to Joining a Gym Challenge

### Objection 1 — "I've tried before and failed, why would this be different?"
The #1 objection. They're not skeptical of fitness; they're skeptical of
**themselves**. Copy must shift the blame off them ("you didn't fail, the
one-size-fits-all approach failed you") and show what's structurally different:
accountability, coaching, a defined 6-week endpoint, a group.

### Objection 2 — "I'll be the most out-of-shape, judged person there"
Fear of the fit, grunting, mirror-lined gym full of 22-year-olds. They picture
humiliation. Reassure: this challenge is *specifically for beginners and people
starting over*, scaled to every fitness level, a supportive group of people just
like them.

### Objection 3 — "I don't have the time"
Real and also a shield. Busy job, kids, commute. Counter with the actual time
commitment (e.g., 3x/week, sessions that fit a lunch break), and the reframe that
6 weeks is finite and doable — not a forever overhaul.

### Objection 4 — "Is it worth the money? / I've wasted money on this before"
They've paid for memberships and apps they didn't use. Money objection is really a
*risk/trust* objection. Address with a guarantee, clear deliverables, and the cost
of NOT doing it (medical bills, another year lost).

### Objection 5 — "I'm too out of shape / too old / too far gone to even start"
Believe they need to "get in a little better shape first" before joining — the
cruel catch-22. And an age belief that it's too late. Counter: meet them exactly
where they are, real people their age/condition who started here, day-one is the
right day.

*Secondary objections:* "What's the catch / is this a hard upsell?", "What if I get
injured?", "What happens after the 6 weeks — do I just bounce back?", spouse/partner
buy-in.

## How to Use This Doc

- **Headlines:** Lead with the identity desire ("feel like yourself again") or the
  blame-reversal ("It's not you — it's every diet you've tried"), not the offer.
- **Lead/body:** Mirror Section 4 language so they feel *seen* in the first 5
  seconds ("If you're sick and tired of feeling out of breath just keeping up with
  your kids…").
- **Bullets:** Convert Section 2 desires into concrete outcomes.
- **Objection section / FAQ:** Map 1:1 to Section 3, starting with "Why this is
  different."
- **CTA + guarantee:** Built to neutralize Objections 1, 4, and 5.
